Run&Gui Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 24, 2026

This English translation is provided for convenience. In case of any discrepancy, the Spanish version prevails.

This policy explains what personal data we process in the Run&Gui application (the "App"), for what purpose, on what legal basis, and what rights you have. Please read it carefully: the App processes health data, which the GDPR treats as a special category and which we protect accordingly.

This version describes the App from the update released on September 24, 2026. If you have not updated yet, the version installed on your device may keep behaving like the previous one until you do.

For any question about your data or to exercise your rights, write to that address.

2. What data we process

We collect only what is necessary to prepare your training plan and follow up on it:

a) Identification and contact data Full name, email address, phone/WhatsApp.

b) Physical and sports data Age, sex, weight, height, goal, experience, weekly availability, paces, heart-rate/watch data, and the training plan your coach prepares for you.

c) Health data (special category, Art. 9 GDPR)

  • The PAR-Q physical readiness questionnaire, injuries, and other health matters you declare.
  • If you give your optional consent to menstrual cycle tracking: the daily entries you choose to record, and nothing else. Each entry is two things: the phase you pick (menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, luteal, or prefer not to say) and the symptoms you tick from a closed list of five (pain, high fatigue, disrupted sleep, GI discomfort, bloating). We no longer ask for or store hormonal contraceptive use, regularity, cycle or bleeding length, the date of your last period, heavy bleeding, or free-text notes about your cycle: those fields were removed from the App in the update of September 24, 2026.

d) Communications The messages you exchange with your coach inside the App and the messages you write in the App's chats (the one that helps you fill in your profile and the one that adapts your plan). Because you write those messages as free text, they may contain information about your health; section 3.1 explains what happens to them and where they go.

e) Technical and subscription data Your account identifier, your device's push notification token, and your subscription status (active/expired). Payment is handled by Apple: we do not process or store your card details.

We do not use third-party advertising or analytics SDKs, and we do not build profiles from your health data.

3.1 What exactly is sent to the AI assistant

We would rather list what is sent than promise you what is not. A promise about what never leaves can break without anyone noticing the day we add a feature; a list of what does leave forces us to update this policy when it changes.

When the AI assistant is involved (generating your plan, adapting it from the chat, preparing your coach's draft reply, or the chat that helps you fill in your profile), the following is sent to the model provider (Anthropic, see section 5), and nothing else:

  1. Your training profile: profile version, age, sex, weight, height, experience, goal, weekly availability, preferences, strength, reference paces and heart rates, logistics and, if you ticked the second box at sign-up, the one about sending to the AI assistant, also your PAR-Q questionnaire and the injuries and health notes you have declared or that your coach has recorded in your medical record. And, if you also turned on cycle tracking, the phase and the symptoms of your daily entries (that closed list of five): when your week is generated, the phase of your most recent entry and the set of symptoms you ticked over the last days travel; and in the Adapt chat your last seven entries, each with its date, travel too, one per line. Nothing from the sign-up form travels any more, because it no longer exists. The assistant uses all of that to write your week with the safety information in front of it. You should know that the safety of your plan does not depend on this sending: the block triggered by an affirmative PAR-Q answer, the brake on pain and the step-down on symptoms are applied by our engine on servers in the European Union, with the complete data, whether or not it is sent to the assistant (section 4).
  2. Your training plan: the overall structure and the weeks with their sessions.
  3. Your recent runs recorded by your watch: date, distance, duration and pace.
  4. Your session reports from the last 28 days: for each day, whether the session was done or not, whether it was a rest day you completed, whether you did another activity and of what type and for how many minutes, the actual duration and distance, your perceived effort (RPE from 0 to 10), any pain you logged (0 to 10 and whether it is getting worse), the note you write on the report (up to 160 characters) and whether the report came from your watch. Effort, pain and the note are what let the following week be built from what actually happened; pain, in addition, is what triggers the protocol that holds your plan back.
  5. Signals computed on your own device from Apple Health or Health Connect data: fatigue, kilometres in the last week, acute versus chronic load, load ramp, weekly readiness and load relative to plan; and, if you have them, your VO2max and your estimated paces. The rest of the raw watch data (beat-to-beat heart rate, sleep, full activities) is not sent: it stays on your device and only the computed signals travel.
  6. The adjustments you make on the Adapt screen: minutes available per day and relative intensity.
  7. The text you write, exactly as you write it: your messages in the Adapt chat along with the most recent messages in that same conversation, your messages in the chat that helps you fill in your profile, and the most recent messages you exchange with your coach (so their draft reply has context).
  8. The text your coach writes about you, if you have a human coach: the instructions they write when they generate or rebuild your plan (for example, "this season prioritise the long run and drop the intervals"), and the health notes they record in your medical record. That text is written by a person, not by you, and it travels to the model provider just like yours.

Read points 4, 7 and 8 carefully, because they are the ones that affect you most. That text is sent unfiltered. If you describe an ache, pain or injury in it, that information about your health is sent to the model provider. This is not a side effect: the system is designed to read it. If you describe pain, the assistant identifies it and the engine uses it to ease, hold back or stop the relevant sessions, which is precisely what makes it safe to adapt a plan in writing.

If you would rather not send free text, you can leave the session report note empty and not write in the chats: your plan is still generated and adapted without it. Bear in mind that, if you ticked the sending box, your PAR-Q questionnaire and declared health notes travel to the assistant on every generation (item 1 of this list), not only when you write. If you left it unticked, or if you withdraw it later, they stop travelling to the model provider, but they are still used inside our service, in the European Union, to brake or block the sessions that warrant it: what the consent governs is the sending, not the safety of your plan.

What is never sent: your name, your email address, your phone or WhatsApp number, and the raw activities from your watch. Those fields are excluded by a closed list in the code itself, which an automated test checks every time we run our test suite.

4. Consent for health data

Processing your health data requires your explicit consent. We collect it in the App as independent consents, and we ask for them before asking you anything on those topics:

  1. Processing your health data (PAR-Q, injuries and health notes) on our servers in the European Union, to build your plan and to put the brakes on it when needed. This one is required to complete sign-up: without the PAR-Q questionnaire we cannot prepare a plan for you.
  2. Sending it to the AI assistant's provider (Anthropic, United States; sections 3.1 and 6). This one is optional and it is a separate box, shown next to the first one and before we ask you anything: you can complete sign-up leaving it unticked, and then your health data does not leave the European Union through this route.
  3. Menstrual cycle tracking, also optional and separate. Turning it on authorises us to process your daily entries (the diary and the step-down on symptoms, which we compute in the European Union). For them to also be sent to the assistant's provider you must have ticked box 2 as well: if you left it unticked, your cycle is not sent to it either.

You can withdraw them at any time by writing to us or deleting your account; withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of prior processing.

And one important clarification, because it is easy to read it backwards: not ticking (or withdrawing) the sending consent stops the sending to the model provider, not the use of that data inside our service. The block triggered by an affirmative PAR-Q answer, the brake on pain and the step-down on cycle symptoms are computed by our engine on servers in the European Union, with the complete data, whether the sending is open or closed. If you also want us to stop processing it, you can ask us in writing or delete your account; section 7 explains what deleting it altogether means if you have the medical block active.

If you created your account before September 24, 2026, you accepted an earlier version of this consent, which was a single checkbox with a less complete text than this one. We will ask you inside the App as soon as you update to the version released on September 24, 2026. Until you do, your health data stops being sent to the AI assistant's provider; your plan keeps being generated and adapted as normal in the meantime.

5. Recipients and data processors

To provide the service we rely on providers that process data on our behalf, under a data processing agreement (Art. 28 GDPR):

  • Supabase (hosting and database). Your data, including health data, is stored on servers in the European Union, encrypted at rest and in transit.
  • Apple (App distribution, subscription payment management, and push notification delivery on iOS).
  • RevenueCat (technical management of subscription status). It receives an identifier of your account and subscription data; it does not receive your health data.
  • Expo (push notification delivery service). It processes your device token; it does not receive your health data.
  • Anthropic (AI assistant that generates and adapts the plan). It receives exactly what section 3.1 lists: your training profile (including, if you ticked the sending box, the PAR-Q and your health notes, and if you also turned on the cycle, the phase and symptoms of your daily entries), your plan, your runs from your watch, the signals computed on your device (including your VO2max if you have it), the adjustments from the Adapt screen, your session reports (with the effort, the pain and the note), the text you write in the App's chats and the text your coach writes about you. It does not receive your name, email or phone number.

Your coach accesses your profile, your plan and your messages to provide the service.

We do not sell or share your data with third parties for commercial purposes.

6. International transfers

Where your data is stored: everything we store, including your health data (PAR-Q, injuries and menstrual cycle), is kept on servers in the European Union (Supabase, EU region). It is not moved anywhere else for storage.

What is processed outside the European Union: some technical providers process data in the United States, and you should know exactly which:

  • RevenueCat, Expo and Apple: account identifier, your device token and subscription status. None of them receives health data.
  • Anthropic: everything listed in section 3.1. That includes the text you write in the App's chats, the pain and the note from your session reports, the text your coach writes about you and, if you ticked the sending box, your PAR-Q questionnaire and your declared health notes; and, if you also turned on the cycle, the phase and symptoms of your daily entries. This is a transfer of special category data and we make it on the basis of your explicit consent (Art. 9.2.a).

These transfers rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914) and on the data processing agreement we have with each provider. In the case of the AI assistant, those clauses form part of Anthropic's data processing addendum, whose contracting entity for customers in the European Economic Area is Anthropic Ireland, Limited.

If you would rather your health information did not leave the European Union, leave the second box at sign-up unticked, the one about sending to the AI assistant. Sign-up completes all the same and your plan is generated all the same: what is required to complete sign-up is the first box, the one about processing your data in the European Union, because without the PAR-Q questionnaire we cannot prepare a plan for you. The cycle one is optional too. If you already have an account and you write to us to withdraw the sending consent, we stop sending that data to the assistant's provider from that moment on; your plan keeps being generated by our engine on servers in the European Union.

It is worth knowing when it actually leaves: only when the AI assistant is involved, and the assistant is only involved on the paid tiers. On the free tier your plan is generated on your own device from templates, and neither your PAR-Q, nor your health notes, nor your cycle reach the model provider.

7. Retention period

We keep your data while your account is active. When you delete your account (see section 8) we delete your data, including health data. If what you want is to withdraw the sending consent without deleting your account, write to us: we will stop sending that data to the AI assistant's provider and will keep it only inside our service, in the European Union, to carry on braking or blocking the sessions that need it (section 4). If you would rather we deleted it altogether, tell us that too, but bear in mind that your plan then loses the information that sustains the PAR-Q block: the block stays on as a precaution and we will no longer be able to lift it, even if you bring us your doctor's clearance. We only keep the minimum information the law requires us to retain (for example, records related to tax or commercial obligations) for the legally required periods, blocked for any other use.

8. Your rights

You can exercise at any time your rights of access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction, portability and to withdraw consent:

  • From the App itself: in Settings › Your account you can export all your data (right of access and portability) and delete your account and all your data (right to erasure). The App does not yet have a switch to withdraw the health or the cycle consent separately without deleting the account: for that, write to us and we will do it.
  • By email: writing to gjcentelles@proton.me. We will reply within one month at most.

Deleting your account does not cancel your Apple subscription; to cancel it, go to Settings › your name › Subscriptions on your device.

If you believe we have not handled your rights properly, you can lodge a complaint with the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (the Dutch data protection authority, autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl), the authority of the country we operate from, or with the authority of your country of residence (in Spain, the AEPD, www.aepd.es).

9. Security

We apply appropriate technical and organisational measures: encryption in transit and at rest, EU hosting, and row-level access control (each user can only access their own data). We do not write the content of your health data to our diagnostic logs: not the PAR-Q answers, not your cycle entries, not the pain figures from your reports, not the text you write. Nor do we use third-party analytics. And since the controller is a single person (section 1), there is no company or team behind it: the database is administered by that one person. If you have a human coach, they see your training profile and the health notes recorded in order to train you, but they do not see your PAR-Q questionnaire or your cycle entries. With one exception we would rather tell you about: when your PAR-Q questionnaire triggers the medical block, the server records in its technical log that the block fired and for what reason, without your name or your identifier.

10. Minimum age

The App is intended for people aged 18 or over. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under that age.

11. Changes to this policy

September 24, 2026. This revision corrects several things the previous version stated that were not accurate, and describes the new consent:

  • The health consent is split into two boxes: processing your data in the European Union (required for sign-up) and sending it to the AI assistant's provider (optional). You can complete sign-up accepting the first and refusing the second. The menstrual cycle one is a third, independent consent, and all three are asked before we ask you anything on those topics.
  • We stopped collecting nine cycle fields in the sign-up form (hormonal contraceptive use, regularity, cycle and bleeding length, date of the last period, heavy bleeding, symptoms and notes). Only the daily entries remain: phase and symptoms from a closed list. We were collecting them without any use beyond sending them, which is the opposite of the minimisation required by Art. 5.1.c.
  • The explanation of consent is corrected. The previous version implied that without consent your plan stopped being safe. That is not the case: consent governs the sending of your data to the AI assistant's provider, not the use our engine makes of it in the European Union, which keeps applying the medical block, the brake on pain and the step-down on symptoms with the complete data.
  • The mention of "Controls mode" as a way to adapt the plan without free text is removed. That option stopped being available in the interface on 2 August 2026, six weeks before the previous version of this policy was published offering it as a privacy remedy.
  • The mention of the EU-US Data Privacy Framework is removed, because Anthropic does not appear on that framework's official list. The transfer relies on the Standard Contractual Clauses, which are part of its data processing addendum.
  • We spell out what is recorded in the server's diagnostic logs (section 9).

September 15, 2026. Section 3.1 is completed with two things the code was already sending to the assistant's provider and the list did not name: your session reports (including the effort, the pain and the note you write) and the text your coach writes about you (their instructions when generating the plan, and the health notes they record in your medical record). This is not a new processing operation nor a new recipient: same purpose, same processor and same category of data already covered by your health consent; what was wrong was the list, which said "and nothing else" without being complete.

We may update this policy to reflect legal or service changes. We will publish the current version at this same address and indicate the date of the last update. If the changes affect the processing of your health data, we will inform you and, where appropriate, ask for your consent again.
